Are the short conspiracy scales interchangeable?

Researchers often treat four short scales of “conspiracy mentality” as substitutes for one another. All four do reflect one common trait, and that trait does most of the work. But two of the scales carry something more, so they are not strictly interchangeable.

Three conditions for interchangeability

A scale can stand in for the others only if it reflects the common trait, carries no extra content that shapes answers to specific conspiracy questions, and scores people the way the common trait does. Hover over or tap a mark for the evidence.

GCB-5: paranormal content

β = .52, .53

Its leftover factor predicted belief in the Roswell and Area 51 cover-ups as strongly as the common factor did. The ACT’s leftover did not (β = .03, −.03). It was also related to odd and magical thinking.

GMC: partisan content

r = .28

Its leftover factor tracked Republican identity and predicted a cluster of partisan-coded beliefs in 2023: SARS as a bioweapon, the Obama “birther” claim, anti-vaccine claims, and climate change as a hoax.

GMC: compressed scores

~1.5 and ~1.25 SD

Relative to the common factor, the GMC places people low on the trait about 1.5 standard deviations higher, and people high on the trait about 1.25 lower.

Item-level loadings (Table 4)

Single items should be read with care. Outside the GMC, the scale-specific loadings were weak and inconsistent in sign, so a large value on one item, such as CMQ.2, does not mean the scale carries coherent extra content. The scale-level results above are the ones the paper’s conclusions rest on.
